Believability

What makes a promise worth believing?

You made a promise to your employees, your customers, your community. You built the program, sent the communications, ran the launch. Believability is the question underneath all of it: have people been given real reason to believe it, or does it only look that way?

Most leadership training on trust starts and ends with communication: say it better, say it more often. Believability starts one layer deeper.

The Sequence

Six stages. Wherever one is missing is usually where the promise breaks.

Sometimes the fix really is the message. Just as often it sits underneath: who has the authority to act, what is rewarded, or whether the experience holds up the same way for everyone the promise was made to.

01

Promise or Commitment

Was this defined clearly enough to check?

A promise that sounds resonant can still be too vague to test against evidence later. Precision here is what makes everything after it possible to examine.

Ask your team: Could someone check, a year from now, whether we kept it?

Tap for a question to ask
02

Conditions

Did we build the budget, authority, and systems to deliver it?

Intention does not deliver a promise. Resources, authority, and incentives do, however sincerely it was meant.

Ask your team: Who has the authority and the budget to deliver this, and do they know it?

Tap for a question to ask
03

Proof

Did people see evidence they could verify for themselves?

Good work that never reaches someone's actual experience is not proof yet. It is still activity.

Ask your team: What could a skeptical employee verify without asking us?

Tap for a question to ask
04

Believability

Have people been given reason to believe this specific claim?

Believability is a judgment about one claim, made by the people the claim was made to. It is earned claim by claim.

Ask your team: Would the people it was made to say we have given them reason to believe it?

Tap for a question to ask
05

Trust

What builds as promises keep holding.

Trust is what accumulates when claims keep proving out. It is a result, not a lever, which is why communicating harder rarely repairs it.

Ask your team: Has this promise held long enough for trust to start building?

Tap for a question to ask
06

Reciprocity

What people may choose to give back.

Belief and trust can lead people to invest effort, loyalty, or support. It is never owed, and never something to promise on their behalf.

Ask your team: Are we expecting something back that we have not yet earned?

Tap for a question to ask

When a stage is underdeveloped, a gap appears: the Composition Gap, the visible distance between what people are asked to believe and what they have been given reason to believe.

This does not assume anything failed. It starts from a narrower, more useful question: which stage has not been built yet, and what would it take?

An engagement score that looks fine on average can still hide a promise that works well for some groups and quietly fails others. That difference often separates an initiative that keeps its funding from one that loses support next cycle.

Dana Middleton, Strategic Communications Leader and creator of the Believability body of thought leadership
