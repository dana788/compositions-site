Three Ways to Begin

Begin with the level of inquiry the situation requires.

Every question does not require the same amount of work. Compositions offers three distinct ways to begin, depending on what leadership needs to determine and how much evidence the situation requires.

01

Commitment Inquiry

Determine the responsible path.

A complimentary, self-guided tool—about 10 minutes—for leaders responsible for an important promise, priority, initiative, or expectation who want language for what they're seeing and a clearer sense of what the situation may require.

This is pattern recognition and routing—not a diagnosis, evidence review, or advisory engagement.

Start the Commitment Inquiry →

02

Executive Inquiry

Answer one consequential leadership question.

A bounded advisory engagement for an executive who needs a disciplined, well-supported response before making a decision, advancing an initiative, or committing to broader work.

The inquiry remains focused on one governing question. It is not a substitute for a full strategy, research, assessment, or implementation engagement.

Discuss an Executive Inquiry →

03

Commitment Coherence Assessment

Examine what is producing the current result.

A formal, evidence-based examination of one consequential organizational commitment—what has been promised, how it has been communicated, how the organization is operating, what stakeholders are experiencing, and what leadership must address next.

This is the appropriate entry when the organization needs more than interpretation of one question and requires a structured assessment of the arrangement behind the result.

Learn About the Commitment Assessment →

These are different entry paths, not stages every client moves through. The appropriate path depends on the question, the evidence needed, and the decision leadership must make.

How the Engagement Takes Shape

Begin with the question. Scope only what the situation requires.

Some engagements remain focused on one executive question or one defined area of work. Others require strategy, communications, operationalization, research, or governance to work together around the same organizational commitment.

The scope is determined by what leadership needs to decide, what is already known, and what the evidence shows the organization must address. The work can remain focused, expand into an integrated solution, or continue over time when ongoing counsel, measurement, or governance is required.

When Several Commitments Interact

Organizations are usually responsible for sustaining more than one consequential commitment at a time.

An organizational commitment portfolio is simply the full set of these commitments an organization is responsible for sustaining at the same time.

Culture, transformation, brand, workforce, values, customer experience, and social impact may be managed separately. In practice, they often depend on the same leaders, authority, resources, systems, capabilities, and stakeholder trust.

Compositions examines what each commitment is producing, how the commitments reinforce, compete with, or contradict one another, and what their arrangement produces for the organization as a whole.
